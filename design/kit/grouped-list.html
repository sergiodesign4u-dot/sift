Rule and anti-rule

Take the pattern

When one list holds more than one kind of thing and each kind deserves a name and a count: connected and available sources, shared briefs and drafts, evidence from Intercom and from a CSV.

Also when there is one group that can be empty. 6.5 shows "Your briefs · 0" over an inline state instead of hiding the group: the gap is visible rather than assumed.

Put the components together instead

  • The heading stands over a block, not over a list of groups: take .group-t--section on its own, as "Map columns" on 3.2 does. It keeps its own margins.
  • The heading sits inside a toolbar: take .group-t--inline; the toolbar owns its spacing (4.0, 6.1).
  • The list holds one kind of thing with nothing to name: a bare .list, as the three account links on 7.0.
  • A modifier form of the heading is never a direct child of .grouped-list: the pattern would add its padding to the modifier's margins.

Nothing else, and that is the rule of a pattern. No colour, no line, no new value: every number above existed before the pattern did. A pattern that needs a style of its own is a missing component or a missing variant, and that comes first, through the five things a new component is.
