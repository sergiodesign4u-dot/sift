Variants and states

One component and three elements: a text field, a textarea and a select share one zone set, so they are one thing with three forms rather than three things. The select adds a chevron, which is an affordance and not a zone. Columns are the three elements, rows are the states input.css paints: rest, hover, focus-visible, disabled. Active is absent on purpose: a field has no pressed state, and a cell filled for the sake of the template would be an invented one. Error is absent for a different reason: input.css does not paint it, so it cannot be drawn from the product's rules (see Technical, "States").

How to use it

When

This product asks a person to type in exactly one place: bringing feedback in, and naming the artifact that comes out. Nothing else in it is a form. That is why the field is not a decorated control here but a plain one: the interesting thing on a CSV mapping screen is the customer's own sample data beside the field, and a field competing with it would be the defect.

The title-inline form is the sharpest case. A brief is named where it will be read, at heading size, with a dashed rule underneath as the only sign that the heading can be edited. The alternative, a labelled field above a preview, would ask the person to imagine the result instead of showing it.

When not

  • To show a value nobody edits: take a mono line of text, or .tag. A field promises editability. The share link is a real readonly field because it is copied; a source status is not.
  • For help text: take the label, or a line under the field. A placeholder is not help, it is an example. It vanishes on the first keystroke.
  • To shrink the field below 44px for density: take .input--dense. It brings the type and the padding down and leaves the target where it is.
Do
A label above, a field at the floor
A label above, a field at the floor, a border that identifies it. The placeholder, where there is one, shows the shape of the answer.
Do not
A placeholder standing in for the label
It disappears the moment somebody types, so the person loses the name of the thing they are filling in, and a screen reader never had it.
