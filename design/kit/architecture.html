Architecture

The rules of the system. This page comes first in the foundations because a system is read from its rules: the audit, the census and the coverage are evidence ABOUT the system, and they belong to the last group. Mixed into one group at the bottom, the rules hide behind the reporting.

Two ladders, and they are not the same ladder

Both use the word "level", which is exactly why they get separated here. One says where a VALUE comes from. The other says what CONTAINS what.

the ladder of tokens
PRIMITIVE  a raw value, no role
--grey-900: #151A1F
81 of them, 36 colour
↓
SEMANTIC  a role, through var()
--bg-surface: var(--grey-900)
29 of them: 22 colour roles, 4 state, 1 depth, 2 component

Two levels, not three. A component token is a third layer of renaming that buys no flexibility, so it appears only where a colour lands on no role at all. Two have been needed: --step-current-line and --tier-mark, each in the semantic block with its light pair and a comment saying why no role fitted.

the ladder of components
atoms  contain nothing from the kit
↓
molecules  contain atoms
↓
organisms  contain molecules, or are a shell
↓
patterns  Stage 09, not before

Three is the ceiling. The deepest nesting here is a dialog holding a form, and a form is already an organism, so both stay at level three. Nesting decides the ORDER inside a level, not a fourth number.

The code is ordered by level, never by purpose. By purpose a button and a login dialog are both "forms" and land side by side, though one lives inside the other. The cost is not aesthetic: an @import sorted alphabetically puts dialog.css before field.css, so the composed thing sits above its own parts in the cascade, and the first contextual fix reaches for !important.

The showcase is read by purpose, and the level travels with it (2026-11-20). A person looking for "the thing that filters a list" does not know it is a molecule, so this stand groups components by category and shows the level as a chip on each page and a column on All components. The category is for finding, the level is for loading; neither replaces the other.

Three surfaces, and a role paints exactly one

The prefix carries the surface. A role may not paint two of them even at the same value, because the thresholds differ, and that is not pedantry: a fill role placed on a 12px bold caption passes as a surface at 3:1 and fails as text at 4.5:1, and a table of text and background pairs never catches it, because nobody declared it text.

PrefixSurfaceFloorExample
--text-*ink: text, icon glyph4.5:1, or 3:1 from 24px or 19px bold--text-secondary
--bg-*fill: page, card, band, badge3:1, WCAG 1.4.11--bg-action
--line-*line: border, divider, focus ring3:1 where the line identifies a control--line-control

This is why the type scale is --type-* and not --text-*: one prefix, one meaning, checkable by grep. Two roles get two tokens even when the value is identical today, and the test is not the colour but the question "can these two places ever part": --bg-action and --text-evidence are both the one cyan, and attribute A2 signed the citation separately from the action.

How the theme is built

RuleWhy
The product ships dark: :root is the dark theme, [data-theme="light"] redefines every role and state token, 29 of 29not one primitive is redefined, which is the proof that the semantic level is overridden on its own
A role or a state token without its pair does not existwrite both in the same edit; the check is a count of the two blocks, run at every critique
The pair is not a mirrorcontrast is measured against the opposite ground, so the light theme takes a different step: the hover goes darker, the focus ring a deeper cyan
Two switches, two keysthe stand's in this panel; the product's in design/_nav.js, applied before the first paint by design/_theme.js. The roadmap has no theme
A theme problem is fixed in the pairnever on a screen, never by a component reading a primitive

A difference written as a contextual override is not a patch, it is an undeclared variant. Either it is a variant, and it gets a modifier and a row in the inventory, or it is drift, and it folds into the nearest declared form. Two of them were caught that way at the Stage 07 critique and promoted to .input--mono and .input--dense.

The state rules, in one place

RuleWhy
A state is a TOKEN, never a stylehover reads a role, focus reads a role, disabled reads an opacity. The price of breaking it is paid three times: the theme, the consistency of twenty components with twenty hovers, and Stage 11, which would start by collecting states instead of animating them
focus-visible, never focusthe ring is for the person on the keyboard and in the way of the person who clicked
A state does not move the elementno shift, resize, transform or new border: a border on hover moves the neighbours by a pixel, and that is the jump. The theme card's 2px nudge was the last, removed at Step 9
disabled dims with --opacity-disablednever repainted by hand; WCAG exempts an inactive control from both contrast floors, and nothing else may sit under its floor
Opacity is part of the inkevery contrast check multiplies the element's and every ancestor's opacity into the colour. A walk that read the colour alone passed the breadcrumb at 3.44 for two steps (Step 9); the system never uses opacity for ink
A snapshot is a differencerest stands live; hover and focus-visible are photographed. Pressed, when it equals rest, and disabled, the real attribute standing live, are a line of text
Only an interactive component gets statesa badge, a heading and a divider do not, and their pages say so in those words. A hover on a badge is an invented role
