Patterns · the second level of the system

Pattern or components

A pattern is a composition that has settled: the same components, in the same order, on three screens or more. It is taken whole, with its wrapper. Everything else is put together on the screen from the components, and that is not a lesser way to build: most of the product is built like that.

A pattern carries no look of its own. It holds the space between its parts and nothing else; the look stays with the components it is made of.

Take the pattern, or the components

Take the pattern when the screen needs exactly that composition, whole. Put the components together when the composition is one-off, or when it comes in another order. The product's own screens, both ways:

Three questions, in this order

  1. 1Does a pattern below hold this exact composition? Take it whole: the wrapper and the parts, as its page shows them. Do not rebuild its spacing on the screen.
  2. 2The composition is right, but a part needs a look the system does not have? Then a component or a variant is missing, not a pattern. Build it first, with the five things a new component is, then use the pattern.
  3. 3Neither? Put the components together on the screen. If the same composition turns up on a third grey screen, it is a candidate: say so in the table below, do not make the pattern quietly.

When a composition becomes a pattern

Three screens, named

A composition is a pattern from three screens, and the three are named on its page. Two show that the composition is possible; three show that it has settled. A composition on two screens stays markup and waits.

The screens are counted on the grey, wireframes/, which is the whole product: 37 pages, 25 screens once a state page counts with its screen. The colour pages are the proof that the pattern holds, never the count: three of five colour screens is "almost everywhere" in a sample, not a pattern.

No style of its own

A pattern is built from components and tokens that already exist. It holds the space between its parts, and that space is cut out of the component that carried it, not copied. It never holds a colour.

A pattern that stands on three grey screens and on no colour screen is still made, because the rollout will need it, and its page says so out loud. Today both patterns stand on all three of their screens in colour.

In the code the patterns live in design/system/patterns/ and are imported after the organisms in index.css; in this panel their group stands after the components. Both read in the order the system is built.
