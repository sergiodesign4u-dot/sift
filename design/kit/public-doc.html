Q3 roadmap: what customers need most

Three themes carry 89 of the 3,750 feedback items in this workspace. Onboarding leads by a wide margin: 42 items across both sources, and it is the only theme that blocks new accounts before they reach any other problem.

Prepared by Alex Rivera, Product Published 4 August 2026 Covers all feedback to 4 August 2026 Sources CSV, Intercom Reading time 3 min

Recommended priority

  1. 1 Onboarding is confusing n=42
  2. 2 Slow export on large accounts n=28
  3. 3 Confusing billing and invoices n=19

Why this order, from Alex: onboarding blocks activation for every new account, so it leads. Export and billing are high volume but affect users who are already working, so they follow.

Themes and evidence

1 · Onboarding is confusing n=42

New users cannot tell what to do first 1. The blank first-run screen is the most cited single point of friction, and several accounts abandoned setup entirely 2.

"I signed up and just stared at an empty dashboard. No idea what to do first."
"My team gave up during setup because the first-run screen was blank with no guidance."

2 · Slow export on large accounts n=28

Exports time out or lag for accounts with large datasets, blocking reporting workflows 3. The complaint scales with workspace size 4.

"Exporting our account data takes minutes and sometimes just fails."
"The bigger our workspace gets, the slower export becomes."

3 · Confusing billing and invoices n=19

Invoices are hard to read and the plan limits are unclear, which drives billing tickets 5.

"I could not tell what I was being charged for on the invoice."
"The plan limits are not clear until you hit them."

How to use it

When

For the brief that a person outside the workspace reads, with no login: one column of reading, no actions. The accent appears on the citation markers and nowhere else, because on a document with no action to spend it on, the citation IS the trust surface (kit.css, 6.4 block).

The key takeaways come first for the reader who will not scroll: the order IS the recommendation. The author's judgement sits apart from the evidence, attributed by name.

When not

  • For the app's own evidence view: that is the theme detail, which has actions and filters.
  • For a legal page: take the legal document.
  • At the marketing measure: that is .public-main--wide, on 0.0, in the marketing shell.
Why this order, from Alex: onboarding blocks activation for every new account, so it leads.
Do
Attribute the judgement
The author's reasoning is named and visibly separate from the evidence (A5). The method line says the same: nothing was written by hand except that paragraph.

Onboarding is the biggest problem for everyone.

Do not
A claim with no marker and no count
Principle 5: traceable or it does not exist. A theme carries its n= and its .cite markers, or the claim is removed.

Behaviour at width

Fluid padding, one container threshold (Stage 10 Step 4, round 3). The column is 45rem (720px). Its padding is fluid: the sides are --gutter-page (16 to 22px), the top grows from 22 to 34px and the bottom from 34 to 44px between a 360 and a 1040 window; until Stage 10 all three switched at an 820 viewport and the sides were 24 above it. .public-main is the place of what it holds (the shared document, the marketing rows, the legal document), so it declares container-type, with width: 100%: a flex item centred by auto margins is sized by its content, which inline-size containment would take to zero. One query, @container (width < 21rem) on the column: below it the confidence follows the theme label (margin-left 0) instead of standing at the right edge; measured, a key row breaks in two under a 320px column.
