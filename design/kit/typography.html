Typography

Two faces, ten steps, three weights, and every specimen is a real line from the product rather than filler. What the scale looks like on a sentence somebody actually has to read is the only thing worth checking here.

Since Stage 10 the scale is in rem and three of its steps are fluid. rem follows the reader's own font size. The three heading steps grow with the page through clamp(), from exactly the Stage 08 value at 360 to their ceiling at 1040, where the page stops growing; the other seven stay fixed, because a body line is made readable by its measure, not by its size. A size never switches at a breakpoint. Drag the window edge: the numbers marked "now" are what the browser computes at this width. The rules and the curve are on Responsive.

Two faces

Inter carries everything a person reads. IBM Plex Mono carries every number and every piece of provenance, because the count is the trust surface of this product (attribute A2) and a number that changes width between rows cannot be compared down a column.

The scale is --type-* and not --text-*. That rename happened while the token file was being written: --text- has to mean ink and nothing else, or the surface rule stops being greppable.

--container-text 52ch, the measure of continuous text since Stage 10: about 70 characters a line

New users cannot tell what to do first, and the empty first-run screen is the most cited single point of friction. A second group describes the same problem from the import side: setup does not make it clear that feedback has to be brought in before anything appears.

The measure moved at Stage 10. --measure-prose, 92ch, held 98 to 122 characters a line, because in Inter 1ch is about 1.35 characters. Its three readers (the scope line, the snippet, the raw item) moved onto --container-text at Stage 10 Step 4, and the token is deleted. The notice band, which had no measure, reads it too, and from round 3 the theme summary and reason (88ch until then), the legal prose and the form. Why 52ch: Responsive.
