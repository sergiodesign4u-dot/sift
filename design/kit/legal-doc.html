The H1 of a legal page has no rule in kit.css: the grey screens set it with wf-h1--doc (24px), and the colour layer never painted it. This page shows it with the screen head's .h1. Which title size a document takes is [?] until a colour screen decides it.

How to use it

When

For a document that is read, not operated: the privacy policy, the terms, the security page. The column is at document scale, not app scale, and the headings open with a rule so a scanned page still has structure (kit.css, "Legal document").

Every section the TOC lists is an h2 with an id. The TOC and the headings are one list read twice.

When not

  • For the shared brief: that is a different document, with its own measure and a trust surface. Take the public document.
  • For a screen with actions: the product shell holds those, and a prose column holds none.
  • For a link inside a sentence outside a document: take .link.

Behaviour at width

A rail beside the prose from a 46rem place (Stage 10 Step 4, round 3). The prose reads --container-text (52ch, about 476px at 14.5px), the longest continuous text of the product. The new wrapper .legal-body holds the opening .legal-prose, the .legal-toc and the body .legal-prose. From a 46rem place it becomes a grid: the contents a sticky rail of 13rem on the left, the prose beside it (backlog B-27). 46rem is the rail, a 2rem gap and the full measure (715.7px, measured) plus a rem, so beside the rail the prose never loses its measure. The place is the public column. In the 45rem column it is 676px, so the rail never shows there and the contents stay in flow; 9.x takes the rail by standing in the wide column, which Stage 12 states on the screen. Crumb against the breadcrumb molecule. .legal-crumb and .breadcrumb do the same job in the same 12px and the same two inks. They are different rules: the breadcrumb is a bar (22px side padding, a bottom border, a 44px link target, an opacity on the separator); the legal crumb is a plain line inside the column (a bottom margin, inline links with no target size). They are not merged here, because the rules come over byte for byte. Whether one replaces the other is a Step 6 question.
