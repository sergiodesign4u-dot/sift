System guide · for a person who did not build it

Why the system is the way it is

Sift is read at 9pm in a dim room by a product manager who has forty minutes and a month of customer feedback. The screen is the only light in that scene, so the interface recedes and the signal stands out. That is why the system is dark: not because dark is current, but because that one scene fixes the product.

The second reason is the product's only claim: every conclusion can be traced back to the customer voices behind it. So the system is built to show evidence, not to assert authority. Every rule below comes from one of those two reasons, and each one says where it was written down first.

How to use it

Four questions, four doors. The fourth is its own door on purpose: a person building a screen for the first time looks for permission, and finds the prohibition only after doing it their own way.

1Which component do I take?The overview, by purpose: what filters a list, what shows a count, what stands while it waits. Every component has a page with its anatomy, variants, states and markup. 2Which composition already exists?Two patterns, each settled on three screens: the Grouped list and the Filtered list. When to take one whole, and when to put the components together yourself. 3What are the rules?Where a value comes from, what contains what, how a role is named, how the theme is built, where a fix goes. 4What must I not do?The nine usage rules: how many of a component a screen may hold, what never stands next to what, what never appears where.

The fourth door, in one line each

  • R1At most one filled .btn on a screen; every other action is .btn--ghost
  • R2One bottom bar: .selbar and .actionbar never on the same screen
  • R3An empty or failed screen (.state) has no toolbar, selection bar or action bar
  • R4At most one .notice on a screen
  • R5A warning or error band never sits on a raised surface such as a dialog
  • R6Exactly one h1, and it names the screen
  • R7A theme never shows without its confidence, .conf: in its row, its head, and wherever provenance names it
  • R8The app header and the bottom tabs inside the app only, the public bar on the public pages only
  • R9At most one phase strip, .steps, on a screen

Each rule with where it was taken from, and how to check it at a glance: Architecture, the usage rules. A screen that breaks one is fixed on the screen, never in the system.
