A navigation carrier is drawn or it is not: there is no half of a tab bar. That is the one thing here the fluid cannot do. Narrow the window past 600px to see it flip.

A media query asks about the screen, a container query about the place. A card does not know whether it stands in a single column or in a grid of three, and @media gives it the wrong answer: a wide screen has narrow columns too. The shell measures the viewport; a component measures its container, and whoever places the component declares the container-type. A screen file never carries @media.

Why the number is written twice, and why that is not two sources of truth. A media query is evaluated before the cascade of custom properties, so @media (min-width: var(--bp-shell-wide)) never matches, and raises no error. The query therefore carries 37.5rem as a literal. The token is the register, the literal is its application, and every @media of the product must give exactly the number in the register: Step 6 checks that by grep. rem in a media query is the reader's default font size, so a reader with a larger font gets the phone layout on a wider window, which is the point: the line still holds the same text.

Container and measure

Two limits, and a limit is not a breakpoint: it never switches, it stops. The page frame stops at --container-page, 65rem (1040px), the one page container the product already had on its marketing page. Continuous text stops at --container-text, 52ch.

52ch, not 70ch. The ch unit is the width of a zero, and in Inter a zero is wider than the average letter: 1ch holds about 1.35 characters. Measured on real product text at 14px, 52ch holds 67 to 73 characters a line, mean 70, inside the 65 to 75 that backlog S5-1 asked for. 70ch would hold 93; today's --measure-prose, 92ch, held 98 to 122. It retires at Step 4, when its three readers move here.
