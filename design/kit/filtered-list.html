Rule and anti-rule

Take the pattern

When a list has a control that belongs to it alone: it narrows the list, groups it, orders it, or adds to it. The toolbar and the list are then one block, and the pattern is what makes them read as one.

The list can be anything a list is: theme rows (2.0), a grouped list of snippets (4.0), the ordered rows of a brief (6.1).

Put the components together instead

  • There is nothing to filter: on an empty or failed screen the state stands alone and the toolbar goes, and the pattern with it. That is usage rule R3, and 2.1 and 2.3 follow it.
  • The "Load more" foot (2.0, 4.0) stands after the pattern, outside it. The list and its foot are a candidate on two screens, not part of this pattern.
  • The selection bar is not part of it either: the screen shell shows it only while a theme is ticked, and on 2.0 it stands after the list and its foot, outside the pattern.

Behaviour at width

The composition has nothing to rearrange: a toolbar over its list at every width. What it adds is the place: .filtered-list is a container, so the toolbar (full row from 39rem, the compact Filter button below) and the theme card (29rem) answer the list, not the window. The list is as wide as the page column on every screen, so neither threshold moved and no screen changed a pixel. A filtered list put in a narrower place on a later screen (a side panel, a dialog) gets the compact toolbar there by itself. Live: the anatomy above, and the toolbar's own pair on toolbar.html.

One declaration, and no new value. A pattern that needs a style of its own is a missing component or a missing variant, and that comes first.
